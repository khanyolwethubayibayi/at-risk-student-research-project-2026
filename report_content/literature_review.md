## Introduction

This chapter presents a review of relevant literature that supports the study on identifying at-risk students using data-driven approaches. This chapter aims to explore existing theoretical perspectives, empirical findings, and methodological approaches related to student retention, student attrition, learning analytics, and machine learning in higher education. By this, the chapter provides a foundation for understanding factors that influence student risk and the methods used to predict and address it.

The literature review is structured into several key sections. It consists of theoretical literature, focusing on established student departure and retention theories such as Vincent Tinto’s Student Integration Theory and Bean’s Student Attrition Model. This is followed by a review of empirical literature, which investigates the application of learning analytics, educational data mining, machine learning approaches, and early warning systems in predicting student performance and risk. The chapter then discusses research findings from prior studies, highlighting common results and model performance in predicting student success.

## Theoretical and Conceptual Frameworks of Student Retention and Dropout

### Vincent Tinto Student Integration Theory

Samoila and Vrabie (2023) identify Vincent Tinto's theory about student departure as a widely acknowledged contribution that explains how students become involved in the academic community and how their degree of engagement affects their decision to continue or drop out. It has been a prominent figure in the study of student dropout. Tinto (2019) mentions that he aimed to explain dropout by linking students’ actions, staying or leaving to the decisions made by the institutions where they enroll. Nuuyoma (2025) study revisits Tinto’s Student Integration Theory as a framework to investigate the factors that influence to doctoral student dropout and to identify strategies for improving retention. Furthermore, Kocsis and Molnár (2025) reviewed Tinto’s study where they highlighted the use of variables such as family background, personal characteristics and prior academic achievement (PAA) as input factors, while also emphasizing the effect of the interaction between students and the university on student dropout and student success.

### John Bean Student Attrition Model

Hobbick (2020) used Bean and Metzner’s (1985) Student Attrition Model which helped to identify variables that affect non-traditional students and their persistence. The study explored data gathered from students who either dropped out or those who failed a nursing exam. Hardy (2023) utilized two renowned student departure theories, namely: conceptual model of nontraditional student attrition by Bean and Metzner; and the theory of student persistence in commuter colleges and universities by Braxton et al., to identify factors that contribute to nontraditional students existing at college prior to obtaining a degree or credential.

## Learning Analytics Theory

Foster and Siddle (2019) investigates the effectiveness of learning analytics for identifying students at-risk in higher education institutions using 2 cohorts of undergraduate first year students. Russell et al. (2020) highlights the advances made by strategies that incorporate learning analytics for higher education institutions to promote student success. A theory informed learning analytics approach was implemented for at-risk student predictions using large-scale behavioral, demographic and academic data (Alhazbi, 2026).

## Early Warning Systems Framework

Bañeres et al. (2020) focused on finding accurate predictive models for identifying at-risk students hoping to reduce the students’ risk of failure. The Early Warning System was identified for its significant contribution to predicting at-risk students and analyzing student performances (Skittou, 2024). Numerous studies have been carried out thus far to identify and keep at-risk students in computer science courses (Veerasamy et al., 2020:1). These studies are relevant in helping to identify relevant models most suitable for handling the at-risk students in the Diploma in Civil Engineering. Identifying the different models will help in selecting the most feasible model for predicting at-risk students in this program.

## Factors Influencing Student Risk

Previous studies indicate that student risk is influenced by multiple factors, namely; a combination of academic, demographic, financial and behavioral factors. Goradia and Bugarcic (2019) found that utilizing a combination of both academic and non-academic indicators provides a more reliable approach to identifying at-risk students than relying on a single category of variables. Similarly, Singell and Waddell (2010) demonstrated that student characteristics/ behavior, academic performance and financial assistance collectively influence retention outcomes. These results indicate that student success is multidimensional and requires that institutions take various predictors into account when identifying students in need of assistance. The relevance of these studies lies in their alignment with the institution's data, which can be used to identify at-risk students and to implement preventive actions aimed at reducing the at-risk population.

## Learning Analytics and Educational Mining

Learning analytics has become a key method for identifying students who are at risk of academic failure and dropping out, with several studies employing this method. Based on multiple literatures, Nik Nurul Hafzan et al. (2019) highlight that predictive modelling enables institutions to identify students requiring intervention before poor outcomes occur. Similarly, Jongile and Ivala (2024) argue that institutional and learning data can be used to develop theory-driven models that support real-time interventions. Cordova et al (2025) highlighted data visualization platforms (e.g., Power BI) and algorithms such as decision trees, Random Forest, and logistic regression as effective methods in identifying dropout rates and at-risk students. Both studies highlight the significance of using analytics to support timely decision-making and student success, rather than solely focusing on prediction accuracy. Together, these studies indicate that learning analytics establishes a foundation for proactive student support systems.

## Early Intervention Systems

Previous Literatures have frequently highlighted early identification of at-risk students, and interventions. Connolly et al. (2017) discovered that when at-risk students were identified early and provided with structured support, academic performance improved. Similarly, Magd et al. (2022) advocate for sustainable early warning systems, academic advising structures and peer support mechanisms. Magd et al. (2022) promote the use of sustainable systems for early warnings, structures for academic advising, and mechanisms for peer support. These findings support the use of predictive models not as an end themselves, but rather as tools to enable early identification for targeted interventions. This aligns with the purpose of the study because the study aims to implement an early warning system while identifying at-risk students.

## Common Predictors Used in Studies

Numerous studies have addressed the global issues of at-risk students. These studies have employed a range of predictors to examine how various factors influence student success and academic outcomes. Predictors employed by Al-shabandar et al. (2019) include; demographics, credits, learning activities, grades, date of registration, date_unregistration, and the number of times the students undertook the course. Latif et al. (2021) used academic results, inclusive of a combination of assessment tools, quizzes, assignments, and tests to predict student results in a university, for any course, at any given semester. Similarly, Nimy et al. (2023) utilized data that included student engagement from Moodle, demographics, and student performance data. The reviewed studies correspond closely with the aims of this project, particularly since the institutional data at hand incorporates comparable predictors.
