## Background

Cape Peninsula University of Technology is a well-established institution in Cape Town South Africa dating back to 1 January 2005 through the merger of Cape Technikon and Peninsula Technikon (Cape Peninsula University of Technology, 2024). CPUT consists of a variety of programs across different faculties and departments. Its programs are designed to equip students with both professional competencies and personal development skills, thereby preparing them for meaningful participation in the workforce and society.

In theory, the university benefits from grants, investments, and reputational growth linked to student success, while students benefit from the institution through access of knowledge, skills, opportunities. To sustain such benefits, institutions of higher learning must meet specific performance requirements, including maintaining acceptable pass rates, retention rates, and graduation rates. These indicators are critical not only for institutional funding but also for ensuring academic credibility and long-term sustainability. However, there are several factors that inhibit the achievement of these benchmarks, particularly at the program and classroom level.

This study focuses on one of the programs (the Diploma in Civil Engineering) at CPUT that has demonstrated low pass rates. By narrowing the scope down to First Time Entering first year students, the study aims to discover the underlying factors that influence students being at risk of not passing, not being retained, and not graduating at the earliest convenient time. Students in their first year who enroll for this program are expected to complete 10 compulsory subjects.

## Research Problem

The Cape Peninsula University of Technology (CPUT) is committed to producing graduates equipped with technical skills and professional competencies for industries. However, it’s difficult to sustain this when challenged by persistent concerns regarding student success indicators, such as pass rates, retention rates, and graduation rates. These metrics are critical not only for the allocation of funds, grants and investments, but also for institutional credibility.

Within the Faculty of Engineering and Built Environment, the Diploma of Civil Engineering has been discovered as one of those programs with the lower pass rates, and graduation rates. The logic of this study is that first year students show more signs of being at risk as they are due transition from high school, technical issues, adaptation, administration, etc. The study identifies students who fail at least 1 subject in their first year to be at risk as they might need to carry that subject to the next year or not progress at all. A study by Connoly et al. (2017) argues that first year students struggle with transition and that may affect their academic performance. To be able to identify these students, it is vital to identify their trends, whether they are in residence, they are funded, etc.

## Research Questions

To address the challenges and underlying factors influencing at-risk students for students in their first year (First Time Entering) in the Diploma in Civil Engineering program, the study develops a set of research questions as outlined below:

- How does availability or lack of financial support influence student success for student in their first year?
- How does residence placement (being placed in residence or not) and financial support influence student success for students in their first year?
- Which subject or subjects contribute most significantly to students being academically at risk?
- Can the available data be used to develop a reliable early detection predictive model for identifying students at risk?
- What initiatives have proven the most effective in addressing at-risk students, and how can these be implemented to the Diploma in Civil Engineering students?
## Research Objectives

This study aims to investigate the factors contributing to student risk in the Diploma in Civil Engineering at CPUT, with a particular focus on first-year (First Time Entering) students. To achieve this, the study reviews existing literature about modelling at risk students and learnt about possible initiatives to handle at-risk students. The study sets to analyze student data to identify patterns in the Diploma in Civil Engineering program. Additionally, the study develops a predictive model that identifies at-risk students at the earliest convenience using available academic student data.

## Research Hypotheses

This study is guided by the assumption that certain student-related features significantly influence the likelihood of academic risk in the Diploma in Civil Engineering at CPUT. Based on preliminary observations and literature, multiple hypotheses are proposed.

Student background (socio-economic factors) are significant determinants of academic risk. Students come from different backgrounds, it is hypothesized that students from lower backgrounds in terms of family financial standing, education level (school quintile), etc., will demonstrate lower academic performance compared to students from higher backgrounds.

Financial status (being funded or not being funded) is hypothesized to have a significant influence on student performance. Students who lack adequate funding will have lower performance compared to students who are financially sufficient. Financial pressures are expected to influence student performance, by affecting concentration and motivation.

Residence status (students who have residence or who do not have residence) is hypothesized to affect student performance. Students without stable residence may be living in environments that are not conducive for studying with possible poor internet connection or no access to internet, inadequate living arrangements for studying, etc. During a period of adaptation particularly for first-year and transitioning students, not having a place to stay may be strenuous, putting some of them under depression, while being left behind on their academics. This hypothesis is particularly relevant given the recurring institutional challenge in which students are left without accommodation, with some reportedly sleeping outside the institution’s facilities. It is therefore predicted that students with secure residence will outperform their peers without residence on measures of academic achievement.

## Significance of Research

The study’s significance is to:

- Develop a model that identifies students who are more likely to be at risk at the earliest convenient time.
- Promote awareness of highly effective recommendable initiatives to handle at-risk students.
- Utilize data-driven approaches to predict student risk levels by analyzing the most influential academic, socio-economic, and personal factors.
- Identify critical subjects/subjects that contribute most significantly to students being academically at risk
- Provide actionable recommendations for educators, administrators, and policymakers to improve retention, pass rates, and graduation outcomes.
